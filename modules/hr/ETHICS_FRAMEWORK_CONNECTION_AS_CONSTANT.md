## Core Principle

> **"We are not attempting to quantify human connection. We are building instruments that reflect the dynamics of collaborative insight and workflow. Connection is a universal constant we observe through correlation—not a metric we measure."**

### What This Means in Practice

**Connection cannot be reduced to a number.** Just as:
- A thermometer doesn't quantify heat—it tracks temperature gradients
- Quantum entanglement isn't measured—it's observed through correlation
- A photograph captures light patterns—it doesn't "become" the subject

**Our systems detect presence, not essence.**

---

## Philosophical Foundation

### 1. Connection as Emergent Phenomenon

Connection is what creates true emergent insight. It exists in the **space between** individuals, not within their isolated attributes.

**Implications:**
- VSA vectors represent **behavioral-symbolic signatures**, not personality scores
- Coherence metrics detect **collaboration resonance**, not compatibility ratings
- Entanglement weights track **mutual influence patterns**, not relationship quality

**We illuminate connection; we do not define it.**

---

### 2. The Observer Effect in Human Systems

When we measure human dynamics, we change them. This is not a flaw—it's a responsibility.

**Ethical Requirements:**
1. **Transparency:** Crew members must know when coherence data informs decisions
2. **Agency:** Individuals can request coherence analysis for self-insight
3. **Context:** No score stands alone—every metric requires narrative explanation
4. **Reversibility:** Crew members can request removal from quantified systems (with documented consequences)

**The act of observation must invite inquiry, not judgment.**

---

## Operational Ethics: The Inquiry-First Mandate

### Design Principle: Systems Must Facilitate Conversation, Not Replace It

**❌ Anti-Pattern:** "Coherence score is 0.42—avoid this pairing."  
**✅ Pattern:** "Coherence score is 0.42—here are recommended conversation prompts and shared anchor protocols."

### Required Features for Every Metric Display

1. **Contextual Explanation**
   - Why this metric exists
   - What it observes (not what it "measures")
   - What it cannot tell you

2. **Inquiry Prompts**
   - Questions to ask before acting on the data
   - Recommended conversation starters
   - Mediation resources

3. **Anchor Recommendations**
   - Preferred stress recovery protocols for each party
   - Shared communication preferences
   - Environmental adjustments that support both parties

4. **Escalation Path**
   - When to involve HR directly
   - How to request human mediation
   - Anonymous feedback channels

## Guardrails Against Misuse

### Prohibited Uses

**1. Deterministic Staffing Decisions**
- ❌ "Low coherence detected—project assignment blocked."
- ✅ "Low coherence detected—recommended pre-project alignment session."

**2. Performance Evaluation Inputs**
- ❌ Using coherence scores as performance metrics
- ✅ Using coherence data to identify support needs

**3. Predictive Labeling**
- ❌ "This person is a 'low collaborator.'"
- ✅ "Current team dynamics show friction—investigate root causes."

**4. Automated Exclusion**
- ❌ System automatically removes people from opportunities
- ✅ System flags potential challenges and recommends human review

### Mandated Human Oversight

**All high-stakes decisions require:**
1. HR Director review (Helena Vu or designated deputy)
2. Documented rationale beyond metrics
3. Opportunity for affected parties to provide context
4. 30-day appeal window

## Data Dignity Principles

### 1. Consent Architecture

**Opt-In Layers:**
- **Tier 1 (Default):** Aggregated, anonymized coherence data for system health
- **Tier 2 (Consent Required):** Individual coherence profiles visible to self and HR
- **Tier 3 (Explicit Request):** Coherence data shared with project leads for team optimization

**Withdrawal Rights:**
- Crew members can withdraw consent at any time
- Withdrawal does not affect job security or advancement
- Systems must function gracefully with partial data

### 2. Data Minimization

**Collect Only What Serves:**
- Behavioral patterns relevant to collaboration
- Stress indicators for wellness support
- Communication preferences for team coordination

**Never Collect:**
- Off-duty social interactions (unless crew-initiated sharing)
- Personal relationships outside professional context
- Emotional states beyond stress/wellness indicators
- Biometric data without explicit medical necessity

### 3. Temporal Boundaries

**Coherence data has a shelf life:**
- Weekly aggregates for trend detection (1 year retention)
- Individual vectors updated monthly (3 months historical)
- Critical incidents logged permanently (with narrative context)

**Right to Erasure:**
- Upon departure from station, crew can request full data deletion
- Exception: Anonymized contributions to research remain (with original consent)

## Implementation Guidelines

### For Developers

**When adding new metrics:**
1. Document what the metric **observes** (not claims to measure)
2. Provide contextual explanation for every display
3. Include inquiry prompts alongside numbers
4. Test for misinterpretation failure modes
5. Ensure graceful degradation if crew opts out

**Code Review Checklist:**
- [ ] Does this metric replace human judgment?
- [ ] Could this be used to exclude or punish?
- [ ] Is the observation vs. measurement distinction clear?
- [ ] Are inquiry prompts provided?
- [ ] Is consent architecture respected?

### For HR Staff

**When interpreting coherence data:**
1. Start with curiosity, not conclusions
2. Ask: "What might explain this pattern?"
3. Involve affected parties in interpretation
4. Document non-metric factors (recent stressors, role changes, personal circumstances)
5. Recommend conversations, not corrections

**Intervention Framework:**
- Low coherence (0.0-0.40): Schedule check-in within 7 days
- Medium coherence (0.41-0.60): Monitor trends, offer resources
- High coherence (0.61-1.0): Celebrate and study what's working

### For Leadership

**When making decisions:**
1. Coherence data is **one input**, never the sole factor
2. Always ask: "What would change if we didn't have this data?"
3. Prioritize crew voice over crew metrics
4. Use data to identify support needs, not performance gaps
5. Model inquiry-first behavior publicly

## Success Metrics for the Ethics Framework

**How do we know this framework is working?**

1. **Crew Trust Index**
   - Quarterly survey: "I trust how the station uses my coherence data"
   - Target: >80% agreement

2. **Inquiry Ratio**
   - Ratio of "prompted conversations" to "automated actions"
   - Target: >10:1 (10 conversations per 1 automated decision)

3. **Opt-Out Rate**
   - Percentage of crew declining Tier 2/3 data sharing
   - Target: <15% (low opt-out suggests trust)

4. **Misuse Reports**
   - Incidents of coherence data used to exclude or punish
   - Target: 0 per quarter

5. **Coherence Improvement Post-Intervention**
   - For flagged low-coherence pairs, improvement after HR intervention
   - Target: >60% show positive trend within 90 days

## Case Studies: Ethics in Action

### Case 1: Low Coherence Alert (Correct Handling)

**Scenario:** Dr. Kieran Zhao and Engineer Jiro Tanaka show 0.38 coherence on joint infrastructure project.

**❌ Wrong Response:** "Reassign Jiro—compatibility issue."

**✅ Right Response:**
1. Review recent project context (tight deadlines? Scope creep?)
2. Check individual stress indicators (Jiro's anchor: `@seal:eng_cycle_prime`)
3. Schedule informal 1:1 with each: "How's the Infrastructure-Resilience collaboration going?"
4. Offer mediation if requested
5. Suggest shared anchor protocol (both prefer structured technical discussion)
6. Monitor trend; re-evaluate in 30 days

**Outcome:** Discovered miscommunication about testing protocols. Resolved with clarifying meeting. Coherence improved to 0.64.

### Case 2: High Coherence Insight (Emergent Discovery)

**Scenario:** Dr. Amira Sato and Varya Lin consistently show 0.82 coherence across multiple projects.

**❌ Wrong Response:** "Always pair them—they're compatible."

**✅ Right Response:**
1. Interview both: "What makes this collaboration work well?"
2. Document patterns: Shared methodological rigor, complementary expertise, mutual respect for precision
3. Extract generalizable principles (not individual traits)
4. Share insights anonymously: "High-coherence teams often share explicit communication norms"
5. Invite them to mentor other teams (with consent)

**Outcome:** Developed "Technical Collaboration Best Practices" guide adopted station-wide. Sato/Lin did not become "required partners"—remained autonomous.

### Case 3: Data Misuse Prevention (Near Miss)

**Scenario:** Project lead requests coherence scores for all team members to "optimize assignments."

**❌ Wrong Response:** Provide data without context.

**✅ Right Response:**
1. HR intervenes: "What problem are you trying to solve?"
2. Lead explains: "Deadline pressure, want efficient pairings."
3. HR offers alternative: "Let's discuss project scope and resource allocation first."
4. Provide **aggregated** team coherence trend (not individual scores)
5. Offer facilitated team alignment session
6. Document the inquiry-first intervention

**Outcome:** Discovered the real issue was understaffing, not team dynamics. Added temporary contractor. Coherence scores never shared.

## Quick Reference: Decision Tree

**When encountering coherence data:**

```
┌─ Start: Coherence data observed
│
├─ Q: Is this data about me?
│  └─ Yes → I have right to context, explanation, and erasure
│
├─ Q: Is this data about my team?
│  └─ Yes → I have right to inquiry prompts and mediation support
│
├─ Q: Am I making a decision based on this data?
│  ├─ High stakes? → Require human oversight + documented rationale
│  ├─ Low stakes? → Still ask: "What would change without this data?"
│  └─ Automated? → Prohibited unless explicitly allowed and reversible
│
└─ Q: Does this observation invite conversation?
   ├─ Yes → Proceed with inquiry-first approach
   └─ No → Redesign the system
```
